# PURPLE HAZE — Art, Audio and Asset Plan

## Visual identity
- Main palette: #10091C, #21132F, #40205C, #853CE8, #BD8AFF, #E0C7FF
- Style: high-quality pixel art with atmospheric depth and controlled glow
- Keep silhouettes readable against dark backgrounds
- Use purple haze as a motif, not as a layer that obscures gameplay

## Character and creature assets
- THE HOLLOW: idle, float, move, jump, fall, land, dash, hurt, attack for each weapon, interact
- HILPO: hover, follow, scan, signal, assist, return
- ECHO: manifestation, idle, dialogue states and disappearance
- Creature set: idle, roam, notice, curious, flee, defend, trust, tame, hurt
- Enemy set: idle, patrol, notice, telegraph, attack, recover, hurt, defeated

## Environment sets
- Shattered Isles: floating platforms, broken ruins, Veil crystals, portal and save point
- Vesper Outpost: exterior, interiors, doors, windows, props, signs and trader area
- Hollowing Caves: cave tiles, passages, crystal clusters, hazards, hidden chambers
- Forgotten Forest: alien trees, roots, flora, water or mist and creature habitats
- Silent City: architecture, interiors, signage, debris and ancient technology
- Distortion: unstable terrain, visual anomalies and environmental effects
- Observatory: instruments, celestial views, archives and ancient mechanisms
- Beyond the Haze: late-game environment language and finale set

## UI assets
- HUD icons for health, Veil Coins, shards and companion state
- Inventory item icons
- Weapon icons and summon effects
- Skill tree nodes
- Map markers and region labels
- Quest and journal symbols
- Dialogue frames, subtitles and button prompts

## Audio
- Original ambient music for each region
- UI and interaction sounds
- Movement, jump, land, dash and weapon sounds
- Creature vocalizations and environmental ambience
- HILPO electronic voice and ECHO voice treatment
- Full voice acting is a production goal; subtitles remain available

## Asset pipeline
1. Define a consistent pixel scale and palette.
2. Create source files with clear names and licenses.
3. Keep editable originals separate from exported game assets.
4. Use sprite sheets with documented frame sizes and animation timing.
5. Compress audio and images for Android.
6. Test every asset at actual device resolution.
7. Record provenance and license for third-party assets. Do not use unlicensed ripped game assets.
